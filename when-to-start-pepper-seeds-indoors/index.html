Why 7 to 10 weeks before the frost?

Peppers sprout slowly and grow slowly, so they need a longer head start than tomatoes. University of Tennessee Extension says to sow pepper seeds indoors 8 to 10 weeks before the last frost, and NDSU Extension's chart gives 8 weeks before the last frost with planting out about 2 weeks after it. Sweet peppers can go a week later than hot peppers; the hottest types are usually the slowest.

How to start pepper seeds indoors

  1. Sow 2 or 3 seeds per cell in fresh, moist seed-starting mix, about ¼ inch (6 mm) deep.
  2. Keep the mix warm, ideally on a heat mat, and evenly moist (not wet) while you wait. Pepper seeds can take two weeks or more.
  3. Move them under strong light the day they sprout, about 14 to 16 hours a day, with the light a few inches above the leaves.
  4. Thin to one seedling per cell and pot up into 3- to 4-inch pots when they have two or three true leaves.
  5. Don't rush them outside: cold soil and cold nights stall peppers for weeks.
  6. Harden them off for 7 to 10 days, then plant out at the same depth they grew in the pot.

NDSU Extension's chart gives 80–85°F (27–29°C) for germination and 10 to 15 days to sprout, longer than tomatoes.

Using your hardiness zone?

USDA hardiness zones are based on average winter low temperatures, not on frost dates, so two gardens in the same zone can have last frosts weeks apart. Look up your own average last spring frost date (your local extension service lists it, and many frost-date tools find it by ZIP or postal code), then count back from it.

Pepper seed-starting FAQ

How many weeks before the last frost should I start pepper seeds?

Start pepper seeds indoors 7 to 10 weeks before your average last spring frost date: sweet peppers 7 to 9 weeks before, hot peppers 8 to 10 weeks before (the hottest types are the slowest). Plant out 2 to 3 weeks after your last frost date, once the soil has warmed and nights stay mild.

When should I start pepper seeds indoors in zone 5, 6 or 7?

Use your last frost date rather than your zone. USDA hardiness zones are based on average winter low temperatures, not on frost dates, so two gardens in the same zone can have last frosts weeks apart. For example, with a last frost on Apr 20, sow sweet peppers between Feb 16 and Mar 2 and hot peppers between Feb 9 and Feb 23.

When can I plant pepper seedlings outside?

Plant out 2 to 3 weeks after your last frost date, once the soil has warmed and nights stay mild. Harden them off for 7 to 10 days first.

How long do pepper seeds take to germinate?

About 10 to 15 days at 80–85°F (27–29°C), according to NDSU Extension's chart, and longer in a cool room. A heat mat makes the biggest difference.

Why do peppers need to be started before tomatoes?

They germinate more slowly and grow more slowly as seedlings, so they need a few extra weeks indoors to reach a good transplant size by the time it is warm enough to plant them out.

Can I start pepper seeds in January?

Only if your last frost is early. Ten weeks before a mid-March frost is early January; with a May frost, January is too early and the plants will outgrow their pots.

Sources
