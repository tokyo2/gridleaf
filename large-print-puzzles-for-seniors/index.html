Large-Print Puzzles for Seniors: Printing Tips & Supplies

Sudoku and word searches are a favorite daily routine for many people, but small print, glare and a wobbly surface can turn a relaxing puzzle into a chore. A few simple changes make puzzles much easier to see and to enjoy, whether you print them yourself or give them as a gift.

What “large print” means

Large-print books usually use type of about 16 to 18 points, compared with roughly 10 to 12 points in a standard paperback. For puzzles, three things matter: the size of each square, the size of the digits or letters, and how dark the grid lines are. A sudoku with big squares and bold box lines is easier to scan than one with the same digits squeezed into a small grid.

Print puzzles bigger

  • Print at 100% first. “Fit to page” can shrink the puzzle slightly; “Actual size” keeps it as designed.
  • Go up a paper size. A Letter page prints at 129% on 11 × 17 inch (tabloid) paper, and an A4 page at 141% on A3. Few home printers take those sizes, but most print shops do.
  • One puzzle per sheet. Zoom in on one puzzle in your PDF app and print the current view; in Adobe Acrobat Reader it's an option under “Pages to Print”. The puzzle then fills the whole page.
  • Print in black. Choose grayscale or black-and-white in the print dialog for the strongest contrast.

Cut glare and boost contrast

Bright white, matte paper of about 24 lb is the best all-rounder: no shine under a lamp, and pencil marks don't show through from the back. Avoid glossy paper. Light matters as much as print size: put a bright lamp to one side rather than directly overhead, so it doesn't reflect off the page, and sit with your back to a window rather than facing it.

The right tools

  • A dark, soft pencil. A 2B pencil or a 0.7 mm mechanical pencil writes darker with less pressure, and a mechanical pencil never needs sharpening.
  • A good eraser. Soft white vinyl erasers lift pencil cleanly without smudging the grid.
  • A firm surface. A letter-size clipboard or a cushioned lap desk makes armchair solving much steadier.
  • Extra magnification. A lighted magnifier helps with small print in books and newspapers.
  • An easier grip. If a thin pencil is uncomfortable to hold, a foam pencil grip or a thicker pencil can help.
  • A highlighter for word searches. Mark each word as you find it, instead of circling, so the letters stay readable.

Tips for enjoying puzzles every day

  • Keep puzzles, pencils and the clipboard together in one basket by the chair.
  • Start with Easy puzzles and move up when they feel quick. There's no prize for struggling.
  • Do a puzzle with someone: one person reads the clues aloud, the other circles the answers.
  • Stuck on a sudoku? Put it down and come back later. Fresh eyes spot the next number surprisingly often.

Large-print puzzles FAQ

What size is large print?

Large-print books usually use type of about 16 to 18 points, compared with roughly 10 to 12 points in a standard paperback. For puzzles, the size of each square and the darkness of the grid lines matter just as much.

How can I make a printable puzzle bigger?

Print a Letter page on 11 × 17 inch paper at 129%, or an A4 page on A3 at 141%. Or zoom in on one puzzle in your PDF app and print only the current view, so a single puzzle fills the whole sheet.

What kind of pencil is best for puzzles?

A soft, dark pencil (2B) or a 0.7 mm mechanical pencil. Both write darker with less pressure, which makes numbers easier to read and erase.

What paper should I print puzzles on?

Bright white, matte paper of about 24 lb. Matte paper has no glare, and heavier paper doesn't let pencil marks show through.
