How to get the most out of it

  • Print at 100% (Actual size), not Fit to page, so every envelope comes out the same size.
  • Plain paper is fine for a classroom craft; thin card stock makes a sturdier envelope.
  • Run a dry ballpoint pen along a ruler on the dashed lines before folding, and the folds come out crisp.
  • A glue stick is enough: glue the side tab first, then the bottom tab, and let it dry for a minute.
  • Fold a banknote once to fit, and write To and From on the back.

The words on the envelopes (Chinese)

The envelopes use simplified Chinese characters, as written in mainland China and Singapore. The pinyin next to each one spells the Mandarin pronunciation.

Chinese and pinyinMeaning
福
fú		good fortune, blessing, happiness
Often seen at Chinese New Year on door signs, papercuts and envelopes.
恭喜发财
gōngxǐ fācái		a New Year greeting wishing you prosperity
Literally “congratulations on getting rich”: a cheerful New Year greeting.
新年快乐
xīnnián kuàilè		Happy New Year
新年 xīnnián is the new year and 快乐 kuàilè means happy.
红包
hóngbāo		red envelope
The red envelope of money given at New Year, and the word for the gift itself.

How to give a red envelope

At Chinese New Year, red envelopes of money are usually given by parents, grandparents and other married adults to children. If you receive one, take it with both hands, say thank you with a New Year wish such as 新年快乐 (xīnnián kuàilè), and wait until later to open it: it's polite not to open it in front of the giver.

You may also see 福 hung upside down on doors. The word for “upside down” (倒 dào) sounds like “arrived” (到 dào), so an upside-down 福 says “good fortune has arrived”. On an envelope it is written the right way up.
