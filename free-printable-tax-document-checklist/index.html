How to get the most out of it

  • Start a folder in January, paper or digital, and drop each form in as it arrives.
  • Compare with last year's return so you notice anything missing, like an account you forgot.
  • Wait until every form has arrived before you file. Brokerage statements often come later than W-2s.
  • Keep a copy of your return and your records somewhere safe after you file.

What documents do I need to file taxes?

These are the forms most people collect. You only need the ones that apply to you, and the printable checklist has a box for each. Report all your income even if a form never arrives.

Also have ready: photo ID; Social Security numbers and dates of birth for everyone on the return; your bank routing and account numbers for a direct deposit refund; last year's return; and your IRS Identity Protection PIN if you have one.

Life changes in 2026 that need extra paperwork

  • Got married or divorced: both spouses' details, and any name change, so it matches Social Security records.
  • Had or adopted a baby: the child's Social Security number (or adoption taxpayer ID) and child care receipts with the provider's tax ID.
  • Bought or sold a home: the closing statement, any Form 1099-S, and records of what you paid for the home and its improvements.
  • Moved to another state: your moving dates, because you may need part-year returns for both states.
  • Started freelancing or a side business: income records for every client (with or without a 1099), receipts for expenses and a mileage log.
  • Retired or changed jobs: a W-2 from every employer you worked for in 2026, and any 1099-R from old retirement plans.

Key dates for the 2027 filing season

  • Friday, January 15, 2027: the last estimated tax payment for 2026 is due, if you pay estimated tax.
  • End of January: employers and most payers must send W-2s and 1099s. Brokerage statements often come by mid-February.
  • Thursday, April 15, 2027: the deadline to file your 2026 federal return and pay any tax you owe.
  • Friday, October 15, 2027: the deadline if you asked for an extension of time to file. It doesn't extend the time to pay.

How long to keep tax records

The IRS generally recommends keeping records for 3 years after you file, 6 years if you didn't report more than 25% of your income, and 7 years if you claim a loss from worthless securities or a bad debt. Keep records for a home or investments until 3 years after you file the return for the year you sell them.

Free printable tax document checklist FAQ

When is the tax deadline in 2027?

For most people, federal income tax returns for the 2026 tax year are due Thursday, April 15, 2027. State deadlines can differ, and the IRS sometimes postpones deadlines for areas hit by disasters, so check IRS.gov and your state's tax agency.

What if I can't file by the deadline?

You can ask the IRS for an extension of time to file, but it doesn't give you more time to pay: any tax you owe is still due by the April deadline. IRS.gov explains how to request one.

When do W-2s and 1099s arrive?

Most arrive by early February. Some, like brokerage statements, can come later, and corrected forms can arrive after that, so it's worth waiting until you have everything before you file.

What documents do I need to file taxes?

Most people need ID and Social Security numbers for everyone on the return, income forms (W-2s and any 1099s), forms for deductions and credits (such as 1098 mortgage or student loan interest, 1098-T tuition and 1095-A Marketplace insurance), records of any self-employment income and expenses, and last year's return. The table on this page lists the common forms, who sends them and when.

How long should I keep tax records?

The IRS says to keep records for 3 years after you file in most cases, 6 years if you left out more than 25% of your income, and 7 years if you claim a loss from worthless securities or a bad debt. Keep records about a home or investments until 3 years after you file the return for the year you sell them.

Is this tax advice?

No. It's a general checklist to help you gather paperwork. Not every item applies to everyone, and some people need forms that aren't listed. For advice about your situation, check IRS.gov or ask a tax professional.
