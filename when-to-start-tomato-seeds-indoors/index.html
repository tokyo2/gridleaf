Why 6 to 8 weeks before the frost?

Tomatoes grow fast, so they need less time indoors than peppers or onions. Sown 6 to 8 weeks before the frost, they are stocky young plants with a few sets of true leaves by planting-out time. Sow much earlier and they outgrow their pots before the nights are warm enough to plant them out. NDSU Extension's chart puts tomatoes at 6 weeks before the last frost, planted out about 2 weeks after it, and University of Illinois Extension groups them with peppers and eggplant at 6 to 8 weeks. Maryland Extension suggests 5 to 6 weeks of indoor growing, so a slightly later start is fine too.

How to start tomato seeds indoors

  1. Sow 2 or 3 seeds per cell or small pot in fresh, moist seed-starting mix, about ¼ inch (6 mm) deep.
  2. Keep them warm until they sprout. A heat mat under the tray helps in a cool room.
  3. As soon as the first seedlings appear, give them strong light: a shop light or grow light a few inches above the leaves for about 14 to 16 hours a day. A windowsill alone usually makes them stretch.
  4. Thin to the strongest seedling per cell by snipping the others at soil level.
  5. Pot up into 3- to 4-inch pots once they have their first true leaves. Tomatoes can be set deeper each time: they grow new roots along the buried stem.
  6. Harden them off for 7 to 10 days, then plant out deep, burying the stem up to the first leaves.

NDSU Extension's chart gives 80–85°F (27–29°C) as the best germination temperature, with seedlings up in about 7 to 10 days.

Using your hardiness zone?

USDA hardiness zones are based on average winter low temperatures, not on frost dates, so two gardens in the same zone can have last frosts weeks apart. Look up your own average last spring frost date (your local extension service lists it, and many frost-date tools find it by ZIP or postal code), then count back from it.

Tomato seed-starting FAQ

How many weeks before the last frost should I start tomato seeds?

Start tomato seeds indoors 6 to 8 weeks before your average last spring frost date. Plant out 1 to 2 weeks after your last frost date, once nights stay above about 50°F (10°C).

When should I start tomato seeds indoors in zone 5, 6 or 7?

Use your last frost date rather than your zone. USDA hardiness zones are based on average winter low temperatures, not on frost dates, so two gardens in the same zone can have last frosts weeks apart. For example, with a last frost on Apr 20, sow between Feb 23 and Mar 9.

When can I plant tomato seedlings outside?

Plant out 1 to 2 weeks after your last frost date, once nights stay above about 50°F (10°C). Harden them off for 7 to 10 days first.

How long do tomato seeds take to germinate?

About 7 to 10 days at 80–85°F (27–29°C), the range NDSU Extension gives. In a cool room they can take two weeks or more, which is why a heat mat helps.

Is it too late to start tomato seeds?

Not if there are about 6 weeks left before you would plant them out. Later sowings still grow, but they go out as smaller plants and ripen later. In a short season, choose an early variety or buy young plants.
