How to price your first digital product

A simple way to choose a launch price without guessing, plus three pricing mistakes that are easy to avoid.

By Folio DemoOctober 28, 20261 min read

Pricing is the decision most new sellers put off the longest. The good news: for a digital product, a price is not permanent. You can test, learn and change it next week. What matters is picking a sensible starting point.

Start from the problem, not the file

Buyers do not pay for “a 40-page PDF”. They pay for an afternoon saved, a plan that finally sticks or a client proposal that looks professional. Write down, in one sentence, what changes for the buyer after they use your product. That sentence is what your price is attached to.

Look at three comparable products

Search for products that solve the same problem for the same kind of person. Note their prices and what they include. You are not trying to undercut them. You are finding the range buyers already accept.

  • If yours does less, price near the low end.
  • If yours does the same job more clearly, price in the middle.
  • If yours includes more (support, updates, extra formats), price at the top.

Three common mistakes

  1. Pricing for your own budget. You are not your only customer. People who need the product often value it more than you expect.
  2. Too many options at once. One product and one bundle is plenty at launch.
  3. Never revisiting the price. Put a reminder in your calendar for a month after launch and look at the numbers.

A launch checklist for your price

  • The price is visible on the product card and the product page.
  • The page says what is included and in which formats.
  • A bundle shows the saving clearly.

Then publish, and let real buyers tell you the rest.
