Write product pages that answer questions

Most buyers leave because a question went unanswered. Here is a simple structure that answers them in order.

By Folio DemoOctober 14, 20261 min read

A good product page reads like a calm conversation. It answers the buyer’s questions in the order they think of them, and then gets out of the way.

The five questions

  1. What is it? One sentence, in plain words, at the top.
  2. Is it for me? Who it helps and the situation it fits.
  3. What exactly do I get? Formats, sizes, number of pages or files.
  4. How do I use it? One short paragraph is enough.
  5. What if it doesn’t work for me? Your refund or support promise.

Use the quick facts

Folio shows a short list of facts beside the buy button: format, size, delivery. Keep each value to a few words. People scan this list before they read anything else.

Show the real thing

Use an image of the actual product, not a vague mood picture. If you sell a planner, show a real page. If you sell icons, show the icons.

End with one clear action

The buy button is already at the top of the page. At the end of a long description, link back to it or to a related product, never to three different places at once.
