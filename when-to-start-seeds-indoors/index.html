How to get the most out of it

  • Find your average last spring frost date first and write it in the box at the top of the chart.
  • Group sowings onto a few dates, say every two weeks, so you are not sowing something new every day.
  • Label every pot or row of cells with the crop, the variety and the date you sowed it.
  • Harden seedlings off for 7 to 10 days before planting out: a few hours outside at first, then longer each day.

How to count back from your frost date

Every seed-starting chart, ours included, counts in weeks from your average last spring frost date. Write that date at the top of the chart, then count back the number of weeks for each crop and write the sowing date in the column next to it. For a last frost on Apr 20, tomatoes (6 to 8 weeks before) are sown between Feb 23 and Mar 9.

USDA hardiness zones are based on average winter low temperatures, not on frost dates, so two gardens in the same zone can have last frosts weeks apart. Look up your own average last spring frost date (your local extension service lists it, and many frost-date tools find it by ZIP or postal code), then count back from it.

Remember that the date is an average: a frost after it is still possible. That is why frost-tender crops such as tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers and basil go out a week or more after it, while hardy crops such as onions, cabbage and kale can go out before it.

Seeds to sow straight into the garden

Some crops grow best sown where they will grow, so they are not on the indoor chart. Roots dislike being moved, and fast crops catch up anyway:

  • 4–6 weeks before your last frost: leaf lettuce, spinach, arugula, peas, radishes
  • 2–4 weeks before your last frost: carrots, beets, potatoes
  • Up to 2 weeks before your last frost: Swiss chard
  • Up to 2 weeks after your last frost: bush beans, sweet corn
  • 1–2 weeks after your last frost: pole beans
  • 2–4 weeks after your last frost: okra

Free printable seed-starting chart FAQ

When should I start seeds indoors?

Count back from your average last spring frost date. Slow growers go first: onions and leeks about 10 to 12 weeks before it, peppers 7 to 10 weeks, tomatoes and the cabbage family 6 to 8 weeks, and fast crops such as cucumbers, squash and melons only 2 to 3 weeks before.

How do I find my last frost date?

Your local extension service publishes average frost dates, and many frost-date tools look them up by ZIP or postal code. It's an average, so a later frost is still possible; frost-tender crops wait a week or two after it.

Do hardiness zones tell me when to start seeds?

USDA hardiness zones are based on average winter low temperatures, not on frost dates, so two gardens in the same zone can have last frosts weeks apart. Look up your own average last spring frost date (your local extension service lists it, and many frost-date tools find it by ZIP or postal code), then count back from it.

What happens if I start seeds too early?

The seedlings outgrow their pots before it's warm enough to plant them out. They get leggy or root-bound and often establish more slowly than younger, stockier plants. If you're early, pot them up into bigger pots and give them more light.

Which seeds should I not start indoors?

Root crops such as carrots, beets and radishes, plus peas, beans and sweet corn, are usually sown straight into the garden. Their roots dislike being moved, and they grow fast enough that a head start indoors gains little.
